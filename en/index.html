How we work

Four things we do differently

We do not promise grades. We promise that every month you will know where your child stands, what they have understood and what they have not, and that no one falls behind without us noticing.

Parents know at every moment what is happening in class

You do not have to wait for a parent-teacher meeting to find out how things are going. Ask anytime and get a concrete answer: what was covered in the last session, what was understood, and what comes next.

A progress report every month

A short document: what was covered, the percentage of the curriculum completed, which chapters need work, and what we do next month. It goes to the parent, not just the student.

Make-up sessions are not lost

A session the student could not attend is rescheduled within the same week or made up online. It is not deducted from the package.

Mock exams under real conditions

For Baccalaureate groups, with timed conditions and the official grading scheme. The mock exam grade goes into the report, so you see the actual progress, not just an impression.

Frequently asked

What parents ask most often

How many students are in a group?

No more than three, ever. This is not a marketing line: if a fourth student wants the same time slot, another group is opened. At three, every student speaks at every session.

What happens if a student misses a session?

The session is rescheduled within the same week or made up online. It is not deducted from the package and it is not lost.

What is in the monthly report, and who receives it?

A short document: what was covered, the percentage of the curriculum completed, which chapters need work, and what happens next month. It goes to the parent, not just the student. For Baccalaureate groups it also includes the mock exam grade, so you see the actual progress, not just an impression.

What does the first session look like?

It lasts 50 minutes, costs nothing, and comes with no obligation. It is not a lesson: we get to know each other, set goals, and see if we click. Only after it do we discuss whether and how to continue.

What is taught?

Sixteen subjects for the National Evaluation and the Baccalaureate, from Romanian and math to Korean, plus five courses outside the curriculum: Excel, accounting, diction, personal development, and financial literacy.
